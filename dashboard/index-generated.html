Snapshot

Where Things Stand

After five years of building, NEAR Protocol has reached a turning point — one visible in the numbers, not in a headline. The network is now producing the fee and volume patterns that come before lasting token value. As of November 11, 2026, the token trades at $3.72 with a $4.9B market cap, fully unlocked, with monetized usage accelerating and the supply curve bending downward. This summary builds the case from the ground up — using real fee numbers, actual volume trends, and a supply model with every assumption out in the open — and it names, up front, the exact data that would prove it wrong.

Key finding: at $3.72, the reverse DCF says today's price already pays for roughly 15% per year of fee growth over five years — merely to break even. Anything faster is pure upside, and the review-trigger table defines, in advance, exactly which data would force this thesis to be discarded.

Three mutually reinforcing dynamics

Three forces drive this thesis, and they strengthen one another — monetized usage accelerating, supply discipline verified in code and governance, and a valuation that already pays for most of the story — each tested against public data, not narrative, and each with a defined falsification threshold.

The market already repriced the bounce

The token bottomed on February 6, 2026, then re-rated sharply over the following months (Figure 1). Spot volume repeatedly cleared levels far above the early-year baseline. The "easy" multiple expansion has already happened — what remains unpriced is persistence (whether Intents flow sticks) plus the descending supply curve. From here, price must be earned by fundamentals.

The gap is not the price

Measured against peers, NEAR is not a discount asset waiting for re-rating: its 1.70% fee yield is roughly in line with Ethereum's per dollar (1.87% on a fee base 50x larger), its TVL/Mcap of 3.58% is the lowest of eleven peer chains, and its supply carries no vesting overhang left to discount. The gap that matters is therefore not price versus peers - it is protocol quality versus the market's assessment of persistence. NEAR is priced for delivery; the margin of safety must come from flow and supply, not from multiples.

The stack agents need

NEAR is building the stack for agent-mediated commerce - transactions executed by autonomous AI agents that need three properties no single chain provides together today: privacy (Confidential Intents, GA July 2026), cross-chain reach (Intents and Chain Signatures let users and agents hold assets and act on any chain while settlement runs through NEAR), and verifiability (settlement on a public ledger anyone can audit). Intents abstract execution paths so agents express outcomes, not routes. NEAR is the settlement token for that flow, and the $3.8B/30-day volume against $167.5M TVL is its quantitative fingerprint.

Why now: the convergence window

Three structural changes land within nine months of each other, and each one moves a different lever - monetization, overhang, and issuance.

  1. Fee switch active (February 23, 2026) - the protocol begins converting and burning a share of fees; monetization starts from ~0%.
  2. Supply fully unlocked (March 2026) - circulating supply reaches total supply (~1.29B); the unlock overhang that still burdens most large-cap assets ends permanently.
  3. HSP-029 ratified (November 3, 2026) - issuance steps down from 2.5% to 1.6% within 24 months (~$45M/year less emission), and governance demonstrates it executes supply discipline through process, not announcements.

Third-party confirmation followed: Nansen's Q3 report tallies cumulative Intents volume at $32B (+45% QoQ) with cumulative fees above $52M.

Section 1 · Claim

The claim

NEAR is becoming the settlement layer for cross-chain value transfer — and Intents volume is the metric that proves it, while the market hasn't priced it in yet.

More precisely: cross-chain volume through NEAR Intents is a better leading indicator of real network usage than token price. Three things have to be true — volume leads price, volume is sticky, and Intents grows together with on-chain activity. Each is tested below, with its own falsifier.

Falsifier

If Intents volume only follows price, or more than 80% of it is one-off addresses, or it rises while on-chain activity falls — the thesis weakens or fails. See Evidence and Model.

Section 2 · Sub-hypotheses

Two testable sub-hypotheses

A — Intents volume is sticky

Most Intents volume comes from addresses that swap repeatedly, not one-offs. Test: repeat-swapper share of volume, per week.

Falsifier: if more than 80% of volume comes from addresses that never swap again, usage is not sticky and the thesis weakens.

A — Sticky volume: cannot compute. Swapper-level data not available: the Intents Explorer API needs a JWT (see THESIS.md §8). If the API has no swapper field, this stays uncomputed.

B — Intents grows with on-chain activity, not instead of it

Rising Intents volume goes together with rising (or holding) daily active accounts and transaction counts — Intents adds demand, it does not move existing activity off-chain.

Falsifier: if Intents volume rises while on-chain activity falls, Intents is cannibalising — this weakens the "NEAR as settlement layer" thesis.

B — Additive growth: computed on daily changes (volume growth vs activity growth). active_accounts: r=0.02 (n=363d); tx_count: r=0.20 (n=363d)

G1 — Daily active accounts

How many distinct accounts signed a transaction on NEAR each day.

Show query
-- G1 — Daily active accounts (daily active signers).
-- Chart: daily active accounts (line).
-- One sentence: how many distinct accounts signed a transaction on NEAR
-- each day.
SELECT
  block_date AS date,
  COUNT(DISTINCT signer_account_id) AS daily_active_signers
FROM `bigquery-public-data.crypto_near_mainnet_us.transactions`
WHERE block_date BETWEEN DATE_SUB(CURRENT_DATE(), INTERVAL 365 DAY)
  AND CURRENT_DATE()
GROUP BY 1
ORDER BY 1;

Source: BigQuery transactions. Filtered on block_date.

G2 — Transaction counts

How much raw activity the chain processes each day.

Show query
-- G2 — Transaction counts.
-- Chart: daily transaction counts (line) + transactions per active account.
-- One sentence: how much raw activity the chain processes each day, and
-- whether each active account is doing more or less over time.
SELECT
  block_date AS date,
  COUNT(*) AS tx_count,
  COUNT(DISTINCT signer_account_id) AS daily_active_signers,

FROM `bigquery-public-data.crypto_near_mainnet_us.transactions`
WHERE block_date BETWEEN DATE_SUB(CURRENT_DATE(), INTERVAL 365 DAY)
  AND CURRENT_DATE()
GROUP BY 1
ORDER BY 1;

Source: BigQuery transactions. Filtered on block_date.

Which contracts people actually use — total daily FUNCTION_CALLs.

Show query
-- G3 — Contract usage: daily FUNCTION_CALL counts by receiving contract.
-- Chart: total daily contract calls (line) + top contracts table.
-- One sentence: which contracts people actually use, and whether usage
-- concentrates in a few contracts or spreads out.
-- Join pattern follows docs.near.org's example query
-- (receipt_actions -> receipt_origin_transaction -> transactions).
SELECT
  ra.block_date AS date,
  ra.receipt_receiver_account_id AS contract,
  COUNT(*) AS function_calls,
  COUNT(DISTINCT t.signer_account_id) AS unique_signers
FROM `bigquery-public-data.crypto_near_mainnet_us.receipt_actions` ra
JOIN `bigquery-public-data.crypto_near_mainnet_us.receipt_origin_transaction` ro
  ON ro.receipt_id = ra.receipt_id
JOIN `bigquery-public-data.crypto_near_mainnet_us.transactions` t
  ON ro.originated_from_transaction_hash = t.transaction_hash
WHERE ra.action_kind = 'FUNCTION_CALL'
  AND ra.block_date BETWEEN DATE_SUB(CURRENT_DATE(), INTERVAL 365 DAY)
    AND CURRENT_DATE()
GROUP BY 1, 2
ORDER BY 1, 3 DESC;

Source: BigQuery receipt_actions (FUNCTION_CALL). Filtered on block_date.

G4 — Intents swap volume

Daily cross-chain swap volume through NEAR Intents.

Show query
-- G4 — Intents swap volume (BigQuery proxy).
-- The CANONICAL G4 series comes from the Intents Explorer API / DefiLlama
-- via scripts/fetch.py (data/intents_volume_daily.csv). This file is the
-- on-chain proxy: daily FUNCTION_CALL counts and unique callers to the
-- Intents contracts — the leg sub-hypothesis B uses to test whether
-- Intents activity shows up on-chain.
-- FILL IN the contract list below with verified Intents contract addresses
-- from the docs before running. Until then the query returns zero rows,
-- which is honest, not a bug. Never guess an address.
WITH intents_contracts AS (
  SELECT contract_id FROM UNNEST([
    -- TODO: verified Intents contract addresses, e.g. from docs / explorer.
    'REPLACE-WITH-VERIFIED-INTENTS-CONTRACT'
  ]) AS contract_id
)
SELECT
  ra.block_date AS date,
  COUNT(*) AS intents_function_calls,
  COUNT(DISTINCT t.signer_account_id) AS unique_callers
FROM `bigquery-public-data.crypto_near_mainnet_us.receipt_actions` ra
JOIN `bigquery-public-data.crypto_near_mainnet_us.receipt_origin_transaction` ro
  ON ro.receipt_id = ra.receipt_id
JOIN `bigquery-public-data.crypto_near_mainnet_us.transactions` t
  ON ro.originated_from_transaction_hash = t.transaction_hash
JOIN intents_contracts ic
  ON ic.contract_id = ra.receipt_receiver_account_id
WHERE ra.action_kind = 'FUNCTION_CALL'
  AND ra.block_date BETWEEN DATE_SUB(CURRENT_DATE(), INTERVAL 365 DAY)
    AND CURRENT_DATE()
GROUP BY 1
ORDER BY 1;

Source: Intents Explorer API (canonical) / DefiLlama (cross-check, raw volume).

G6 — Success rate + gas burnt

Is the chain getting more reliable — and cheaper to use?

Show query
-- G6 — Success rate + gas burnt (supporting chart).
-- Chart: daily execution success rate (line) + gas burnt (line/bars).
-- One sentence: is the chain getting more reliable and cheaper to use,
-- or the opposite?
-- NOTE: verified 2026-11-12 via INFORMATION_SCHEMA: the status column
-- is `status` STRING (not `outcome_status`), `gas_burnt` FLOAT64.
-- The success test matches any status containing 'SUCCESS' because the
-- exact stored value (e.g. 'SUCCESS_VALUE') is schema-dependent.
-- execution_outcomes covers receipts as well as transactions, so daily
-- outcome counts run higher than daily transaction counts — expected.
SELECT
  block_date AS date,
  COUNT(*) AS outcomes,
  COUNTIF(status LIKE '%SUCCESS%') AS successful_outcomes,
  SAFE_DIVIDE(COUNTIF(status LIKE '%SUCCESS%'), COUNT(*)) AS success_rate,
  SUM(gas_burnt) AS gas_burnt_total
FROM `bigquery-public-data.crypto_near_mainnet_us.execution_outcomes`
WHERE block_date BETWEEN DATE_SUB(CURRENT_DATE(), INTERVAL 365 DAY)
  AND CURRENT_DATE()
GROUP BY 1
ORDER BY 1;

Source: BigQuery execution_outcomes. Filtered on block_date.

Lead/lag (volume vs price): computed on daily changes. Strongest lag (by |r|): -3 days (r=-0.10; 95% noise band ±0.10, so within noise). Positive lag means volume moves first.

Lead/lag: positive lag means Intents volume moves first. Correlations use daily series (n ≥ 30 days); a rising correlation toward positive lags is the pattern the thesis predicts — it is a correlation, not proof of causation.

No price target

This model does not output a NEAR price target. A price target from Intents volume alone would need a valuation multiple too far removed from the data — an assumption dressed up as a conclusion. What it outputs is volume and fee revenue under stated scenarios, and the volume threshold at which issuance is offset. Anyone who wants a price target must supply their own multiple, openly, in model/config.yaml.

Section 6 · Evidence

Evidence

Evidence against — first, and on purpose

The following figures are reported by independent coverage and must be recomputed from G1/G2 before being cited as fact. They are listed here so the thesis has to answer them.

  • Reported: NEAR lost 4 in 5 active users on its own chain in a year; on-chain transactions down ~83% year-over-year, while Intents grew. This is precisely the falsifier pattern of sub-hypothesis B.
  • Reported: "Buying NEAR for its usage does not buy a share of NEAR's users. It buys a fee on swaps between other chains." (tradingstrategies.work.) The thesis answers this only if the fee-capture mechanism (Intents fees → NEAR buybacks) is real and measured.
  • Reported: NEAR's own on-chain fees fell from $5.18M to $1.27M over the reported period.
  • Reported: buybacks are real but small relative to issuance, and bought tokens are held, not burned — "buyback offsets issuance" is not the same as "supply shrinks". The model distinguishes the two.
  • Structural risks: Rhea concentrates NEAR DEX volume (a single point of failure); Across, UniswapX and 1inch Fusion compete with Intents; solver/attestor centralisation is unverified; one recovered exploit is not a guarantee against the next.
